Title: The Fed Attention Mechanism (Job Market Paper)
Status: Draft coming soon
Abstract: Does Fed communication make the private sector better informed? I measure how markets divide their attention between Fed communications and other economic information using over 300,000 reports from professional macroeconomic forecasters. I develop a model in which Fed communications shape information processing costs and determine forecasters’ allocation of attention. Supporting this framework, I find that Fed communications shift attention toward the Fed and the topics it emphasizes, crowding out independent economic research. As forecasters shift attention toward the Fed, they rely more on its communications to form their own economic expectations. Attention to the Fed improves the accuracy of short-rate forecasts but worsens forecasts of economic outcomes and longer-term rates. I next show that asset prices react to the magnitude of Fed communication surprises, measured across forecasters, particularly surprises about the Fed’s economic outlook. These findings suggest that Fed communications make the private sector better informed about near-term interest rates, but less informed about the economy and longer-term rates.
