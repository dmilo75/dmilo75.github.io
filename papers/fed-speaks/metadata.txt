Title: Fed Communication and Market Attention (Job Market Paper)
Status: Draft coming soon
Abstract: Does Fed communication make the private sector better informed? I measure how markets divide their attention between Fed communications and other economic information using over 300,000 reports from professional macroeconomic forecasters. I develop a model in which Fed communications change the cost of researching the Fed and thereby shape forecasters’ attention allocation. Supporting this framework, I find that Fed communications shift attention toward the Fed and the topics it emphasizes, crowding out independent economic research. This improves the accuracy of their Fed funds forecasts, but worsens the accuracy of their forecasts for economic outlooks and medium- to longer-term Treasury yields. Consistent with attention crowdout, forecasters also incorporate Fed information into their own economic expectations. I next show that asset price reactions depend on which aspects of Fed communications differ from forecasters’ expectations, especially communications about the Fed’s economic outlook. These findings suggest that Fed communications may improve the accuracy of near-term interest rate forecasts at the cost of reducing the accuracy of forecasts for economic fundamentals and longer-term rates.
