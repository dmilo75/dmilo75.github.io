Abstract: Does Fed communication make the private sector better informed? I measure how markets divide their attention between researching the Fed and independent economic research using over 300,000 reports from professional macroeconomic forecasters. I develop a model in which Fed communications can crowd in economic research by clarifying Fed policy, or crowd it out by making Fed research more productive. Consistent with crowding out, I find that Fed communications shift attention toward the Fed and the economic topics it emphasizes. This improves forecasters' expectations of near-term policy, but worsens their expectations of economic outcomes and Treasury yields. Fed communications also draw forecasters’ economic expectations toward the Fed’s by shifting their focus towards the information it emphasizes. This mechanism helps explain reactions of consensus expectations and asset prices to Fed communications. These findings suggest that Fed communications improve understanding of policy while crowding out independent analysis of the economy.
