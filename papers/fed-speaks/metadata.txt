Title: Fed Communication and Market Attention (Job Market Paper)
Link: https://fomc.dan-milo.com
Abstract: Does Fed communication make the private sector better informed? I measure how markets divide their attention between Fed communications and other economic information using over 300,000 reports from professional macroeconomic forecasters. I develop a model in which Fed communications can crowd in economic research by reducing the need to research the Fed, or crowd it out by making Fed research less costly. Consistent with crowding out, I find that Fed communications shift attention toward the Fed and away from independent economic research. This improves the accuracy of their Fed funds forecasts, but worsens the accuracy of their forecasts for economic outlooks and medium- to longer-term Treasury yields. Fed communications also draw forecasters’ economic expectations toward the Fed’s by shifting their focus towards the topics it emphasizes. I further show how this mechanism helps explain consensus expectations and asset price reactions to Fed communications. These findings suggest that Fed communications improve understanding of policy while crowding out independent analysis of the economy.
